Western digital-speech regulation - the lawful-content control layer

(Part of the speech-control apparatus layer. The state-law counterpart to the payment-processor chokepoint: where card networks gate the money, these regimes gate the content - and both route through the same age-verification / Digital-ID plumbing. Framing is contested throughout; both sides are presented.)

United Kingdom - Online Safety Act + Ofcom (fact of the regime; framing contested)

The OSA 2023 is enforced by Ofcom. From 25 July 2025, sites hosting pornography (and services carrying suicide / self-harm / eating-disorder material) must deploy "highly effective age assurance"; penalties reach GBP 18m or 10% of global turnover plus ISP-blocking. Ofcom cited evidence that 8% of UK children aged 8-14 saw porn online in a single month. The rollout drove a VPN-download surge and a Wikimedia Foundation judicial review of the "Category 1" duties - dismissed 11 Aug 2025, though the court warned the ruling was not a green light to impede Wikipedia. Defenders: child protection + platform accountability. Critics (incl. Al Jazeera, EFF-aligned voices): age-gating legal speech and building an identity chokepoint.

European Union - the Digital Services Act + "Chat Control"

  • DSA: Commission-enforced against Very Large Online Platforms (open probes into X, Meta, TikTok), fines up to 6% of global turnover, systemic-risk + trusted-flagger + transparency duties. US officials (Vance) and Musk call it EU censorship pressure; the Commission calls it illegal-content accountability. Contested.
  • Chat Control (CSAR): the interim voluntary-scanning derogation narrowly survived a 9 Jul 2026 vote - 314 MEPs voted to scrap it vs 276 to keep, but 360 were needed to block the Council, so it stands to 2028. The permanent regime - which several drafts would turn into client-side scanning - was renegotiated from Sep 2026. Critics (Patrick Breyer) call it "suspicionless mass surveillance"; the Commission's own report on the expired derogation cited false positives as high as 20%. This is the frontier that reaches into private, encrypted messaging, not just published content.

Germany - NetzDG (the template)

The 2017 NetzDG ("24-hour takedown or fine") was the pioneering platform-speech-liability law and the historical ancestor of the EU regime; it was largely absorbed into the DSA in 2024.

France - SREN law + Arcom

The SREN law (Loi 2024-449, 21 May 2024) empowers Arcom (merged CSA+HADOPI) to enforce porn-site age verification. Since 11 Apr 2025 an accepted method must meet "double anonymity" (the site never sees identity; the verifier never sees the site) - card-only no longer qualifies. Arcom can order ISP/DNS blocking within 48h (upheld by the Conseil d'Etat); penalties up to EUR 150k or 2% turnover. Litigation + CJEU referrals are ongoing.

Australia - eSafety + the under-16 ban

The Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act made Australia the first country to enforce an under-16 social-media ban (live 10 Dec 2025). In scope: FB/IG/TikTok/Snap/X/YouTube/Twitch/Reddit/Kick/Threads; out of scope: Steam/Discord/WhatsApp/messaging/gaming. Onus is on platforms (fines up to AUD 49.5m); Meta began removing under-16s from 4 Dec 2025 via face-scan or ID. Phase-2 codes extend age assurance to search / app-stores / messaging through Mar 2026. Even the eSafety Commissioner called it a "very blunt approach" (Jun 2026), and PM Albanese floated tightening it further.

"Child safety" age-verification is the most politically durable on-ramp to pervasive age + identity attestation - and Phase-2 / app-store laws push the check down to the device/OS (Apple/Google age signals), moving the chokepoint from the website to the phone.
