Fed hikes +25bps (Sep 2026) - first increase since 2023

On 16 Sep 2026 the FOMC raised the federal-funds target range +25bps to 3.75-4.00% - the first hike since 2023 - by a unanimous 12-0 vote. That unanimity reversed July's contentious 9-3 hold, when three members had dissented in favor of a hike. Stated rationale: elevated inflation (an oil-price spike / war) plus bond-market pressure (the funds rate had sat unusually far below traded yields). The dot plot flags another hike possible by yearend (4.00-4.25%), with 2027 split - a sharp turn from March's projected cuts ("a war and six months").

Why it matters across the map (transmission)

The hike is not a standalone macro fact - it reprices several threads at once:

  • AI-datacenter debt (costlier). The SPV + private-credit wave (spec-ai-datacenter-debt-spv) reprices higher: floating-rate facilities (CoreWeave's ~11% GPU-collateralized loans) and new SPV debt priced off Treasuries both get more expensive - tightening the debt-funded buildout and worsening the depreciation/duration-mismatch trap.
  • SINK / bond market (tighter). Higher discount + refinancing costs squeeze leveraged, negative-cash-flow borrowers.
  • Stablecoins (tailwind). The mirror image: higher T-bill yields fatten issuer reserve income on the float - Circle, Tether, USD1, Arc, USDtb all earn more carry. The same higher-for-longer that hurts borrowers pays the issuers.
  • Household credit (squeezed). Higher-for-longer compounds the subprime auto/card stress (macro-consumer-abs-subprime) at an already-strained consumer bottom.
