Elon Musk back in government - Pentagon "Project Meridian"

(Defense / state-capital thread. A year after DOGE, Musk returns to a government role - this time inside the Pentagon, co-directing a study of the weapons the US should build next, while his own companies are among the biggest sellers of exactly those weapons.)

What it is (fact)

On 30 September 2026, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced Project Meridian - a 120-day study of the future of warfare - during his "State of the Force" address at MCB Quantico. It is co-directed by Elon Musk, Anduril co-founder Palmer Luckey, and former House Speaker Newt Gingrich, with a panel of "elite academic, policy, and industry experts."

  • Mandate: identify the capabilities needed for "absolute technological dominance" on next-generation battlefields - AI, autonomy, directed energy, robotics, biotech - across "the full spectrum of future combat environments, from underground depths to lunar space."
  • Deliverable: recommendations to Hegseth's office no later than 28 January 2027, released as an unclassified report.

The context (fact)

  • Musk previously ran DOGE (Department of Government Efficiency) as a special government employee under the 130-day statutory limit, then left the White House and publicly fell out with Trump. Meridian is his return.
  • His companies are already major Pentagon contractors: SpaceX is building satellite layers for the "Golden Dome" missile-defense shield; xAI's Grok was approved for use on classified systems in 2026.

The structural conflict (labeled interpretation)

Meridian's job is to recommend which next-gen weapons the military should fund. Its co-directors run firms that sell those exact categories: Luckey -> Anduril (autonomy/defense-tech prime), Musk -> SpaceX (space/missile-defense) and xAI (military AI). This is a documented conflict-of-interest concern - a panel shaping the demand curve its members' companies supply - not an allegation of wrongdoing. The honest point is the structure, which the map tracks the same way it tracks regulatory capture elsewhere.

Honest limits
