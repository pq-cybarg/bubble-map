weak.intel · a dual-use landscape

One stack.
Two faces.

The cyber landscape is not two armies. It is a single substrate — cryptographic primitives, vendor products, identity planes, lawful-intercept doors — that APTs, criminals, agencies, and blue teams all stand on. Recurring compromise happens where that substrate concentrates. A CRQC is the forcing event that reprices every recorded RSA/ECC ciphertext (harvest-now-decrypt-later) and every classical signature (trust-now-forge-later).

The story we can already tell

  1. The dual-use door. CALEA lawful intercept was built for US law enforcement; Salt Typhoon used the same door. Dual_EC sat in a NIST standard for years. Crypto AG sold the world's ciphers while its owners read them. A shared channel cannot be proven exclusive — that is a theorem (S2), not a vibe.
  2. Leak and boomerang. EternalBlue left a hoard and came back as WannaCry and NotPetya. TLA+ Inv_NoCascade is written to be violated.
  3. Weaponize the guard. DoubleAgent and BYOVD turn EDR into the attack surface. “EDR is running” is not a safety invariant if the process is injectable (B2).
  4. CRQC as the unwind. HNDL means the confidentiality loss is already booked. TNFL means identity credentials issued on ECDSA become impersonable at Q-Day. Silent-Q-Day is graded speculation: the first CRQC owner has a reason not to announce.
  5. Launder and vanish. Crypto mixers are the settlement rail of the same substrate: Lazarus washed stolen funds through Tornado Cash, yet the mixer is also a genuine privacy tool — so Tornado_Cash, Samourai_Wallet, and Financial_Privacy surface as dual-use nodes on their own, not by assertion. The accountability split is honest: OFAC's Tornado Cash designation was judicially vacated (the immutable code got away) while its developer Roman Storm did not — the protocol outlived the sanction, the person did not.
  6. The marketplace tier. eCrime is not just crews and malware; it is a wholesale economy. Initial Access Brokers sell the foothold; darknet markets (Silk Road, AlphaBay, and their continuous rebirths) move stolen data and access. Takedowns kill instances, not the model — which is why the class node persists while the instances land in did-not-get-away.
  7. What still works. Segmentation, immutable backups, memory-safe rewrites, AES-256, ML-KEM, PPL on security services. They work because they leave the shared public-key substrate or shrink the blast radius — not because they paint over it.
