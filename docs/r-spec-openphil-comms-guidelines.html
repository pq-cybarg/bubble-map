Open Phil / EAF 2019 communication guidelines

On 8 Oct 2026, David Z. Morris (@davidzmorris) posted a six-part thread and a Zero Knowledge article arguing that "AI Safety" research is a hallucination and that a 2019 Open Philanthropy grant to the Effective Altruism Foundation (EAF) came with unpublished communication guidelines that, in his telling, told grantees to cite and echo Open Phil leaders. The X post is a lead. The overlay below is sourced from the guideline PDFs and Knutsson writeup, the Open Phil grant record, Cremer/Kemp 2021, Cremer's EA Forum post and the public replies, Oxford's FHI close, and Oxford's Research Integrity Policy as public text. Morris's headline is journalist framing, not a graph fact.

This block sits next to catalog-nonprofits-foundations (Open Phil as EA grantmaker; Open Phil -> OpenAI 2017) and spec-buist-v-anthropic-pacing (do not blob Coefficient/EA into Hunt or TL4J). Coefficient Giving and Open Philanthropy stay two philanthropy nodes. Overlay. Excluded from proofs.

1. The two guideline documents (fact of the PDFs Knutsson preserved)

18 Jul 2019. Stefan Torges (then EAF co-executive director) shared two Google docs with Simon Knutsson, then a university PhD student and former Foundational Research Institute (FRI) researcher who had been offered EAF/FRI funding.

  1. Nick Beckstead, "Guidelines for Discussing Longtermism and Existential Risks," version 1.6, last updated 3 Jul 2019. Header: written by Beckstead; endorsed by 80,000 Hours, CEA, CFAR, MIRI, Open Philanthropy, Nick Bostrom, Will MacAskill, Toby Ord, Carl Shulman. Marked not for publication; share via Bastian Stern (bastian@openphilanthropy.org). (PDF)
  2. EAF, "Guidelines for Discussing Astronomical Stakes," first written Mar 2019, last updated 10 Sep 2019. Authors named in Knutsson's copy: Jonas Vollmer, Stefan Torges, Lukas Gloor, David Althaus. "Ask Stefan before sharing." (PDF)

Beckstead's substance, as the PDF states it: go out of the way to highlight beliefs that matter to suffering-focused ethics (SFE); acknowledge that the future might be highly suboptimal or contain tragedy even if extinction is avoided; do not leave readers thinking longtermism is only extinction-risk work, or that the future is inevitably near-optimal. Written in response to EAF's own SFE-communication draft.

EAF's document, as Knutsson quotes it, encourages following the guidelines for all public communication, including scholarly publications; discourages writing that the future will likely contain more disvalue than value; recommends referencing Beckstead's dissertation and texts by Carl Shulman, MacAskill, and Bostrom.

Morris's phrase "echo Open Phil leaders" is a journalist characterization of that citation list plus grant timing. It is not a verbatim Beckstead instruction to echo Open Phil as an institution.

Knutsson's own pages: Problems in EA and existential risk (16 Oct 2019, last update 1 Jul 2022) and the email exchange with Open Phil (10 Nov 2019).

2. The $1M grant (fact of the grant record)

Open Philanthropy granted $1,000,000 to the Effective Altruism Foundation in July 2019, announced about 2019-07-30, duration 24 months, purpose "to support research and operations." Investigators: Nick Beckstead and Claire Zabel. Cause area: Effective altruism.

The live Coefficient Giving grants path 404s. The historical Open Phil URL was https://www.openphilanthropy.org/giving/grants/effective-altruism-foundation-research-operations. The text is still quoted by Knutsson and indexed at vipulnaik's donor-donee dump.

Grant-page language Knutsson quotes (treat as the public rationale, not a secret appendix):

  • "A major purpose of this grant is to encourage and support EAF and our other grantees in the space in taking approaches to longtermism with greater emphasis on shared objectives between different value systems."
  • "EAF is an organization whose values put a particular emphasis on trying to reduce the risks of future suffering. While preventing suffering is a value we share, we also believe that the speculative and suffering-focused nature of this work means that it needs to be communicated about carefully, and could be counterproductive otherwise. As a result, we have felt ambivalent about EAF's work to date..."

Michael Levine (Open Phil communications) told Knutsson on 5 and 8 Nov 2019 that they did not have anything to add beyond the grant page. Beckstead did not reply to Knutsson.

Knutsson's understandings, not Open Phil confirmation:

  • "Based on a conversation, I gather the grant might not have been made if EAF did not write their guidelines."
  • Torges used the phrases "coordinated effort" and "we will promote future pessimism ... to a lesser extent."
  • "I was also told essentially that I could likely get funding in the future if I would follow these guidelines."
  • The guidelines were binding for people employed or funded by EAF/FRI and "also meant for independent researchers and university employees."

Those four sentences are Knutsson/Torges characterization. They are not a signed employment contract, not an Oxford finding, and not an Open Phil press line.

3. Cremer / Kemp 2021 (fact of the paper and the Forum thread)

Carla Zoe Cremer, former Future of Humanity Institute Research Scholar (CSER affiliate; FHI DPhil scholarship listed from fall 2021 on contemporaneous bios), co-authored with Luke Kemp:

  • Democratising Risk: In Search of a Methodology to Study Existential Risk, arXiv 2201.11214 / SSRN 3995225, 27-28 Dec 2021. Critiques a "techno-utopian approach" associated with the MacAskill / Bostrom / Ord wing. Later an Open Book Publishers chapter (2024).
  • EA Forum, 28 Dec 2021: Democratising Risk - or how EA deals with critics.

Cremer's own words on that post (fact of the post, not a finding of retaliation):

"The creation of this paper has not signalled epistemic health... most emotionally draining paper we have ever written... Making the case for democracy was heavily contested... these same people that then tried to prevent this paper from being published. They did so largely out of fear that publishing might offend key funders who are aligned with the TUA... concerned that any critique of central figures in EA would result in an inability to secure funding from EA sources, such as OpenPhilanthropy. We don't know if these concerns are warranted."

Public replies, 28-29 Dec 2021 (fact of the thread):

  • Will MacAskill (301): sorry; offers to fund red-team work.
  • Nick Beckstead (278): "raising these criticisms wouldn't count as a black mark... I disagree with the idea that publishing critiques like this is dangerous."
  • Holden Karnofsky (188): "Whoever told you that funders would be upset by your publishing this piece, they didn't speak for Open Philanthropy."

Morris treats Cremer as the dissent that the 2019 comms machine was built to contain. The documents show reviewer fear of Open Phil, named by Cremer, plus public Open Phil/EA principals saying they would not punish the paper. Career-harm intimations are Cremer's report of that fear. They are not a proven Open Phil retaliatory act.

FHI was shuttered by the University of Oxford on 16 Apr 2024. ZK says Cremer's FHI appointment ended in 2021; some directories still listed her as an FHI scholar through institute close. Grade the end-date as disputed in secondary listings; the Research Scholar affiliation itself is documented.

4. Oxford ethics (policy text vs allegation)

Oxford's Research Integrity Policy, as public university text, includes 2.2 academic freedom and 4.2 integrity breaches that include suppression of inconvenient results. Morris/ZK allege that Oxford staffers (Bostrom, Ord, MacAskill) endorsing unpublished EA communication guidelines may conflict with 2.2 / 3.2 / 4.2. ZK contacted Oxford; no response at article time.

Endorsement of informal EA comms guidelines is not a finding of misconduct. "Violates Oxford ethics" is a Morris/ZK allegation, and a Knutsson reading of Torges's "university employees" line. This overlay does not graph an Oxford verdict.
