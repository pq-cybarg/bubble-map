# Commodity traders - the Russian-oil price cap, the shadow fleet, and the bribery track (kept separate)

*(Deepens the commodity-trading-houses batch. Two DISTINCT issues people often blur: Russia-sanctions compliance, and the Latin-America bribery settlements. This block keeps them apart on purpose.)*

## The price cap (fact)
The G7/EU **price cap** on seaborne Russian oil: **Dec 2022 - $60** Urals (+$100/$45 products); **late 2025** the EU switched to a **dynamic formula** (~15% below the 6-month average, ~$48); **mid-2026** it **froze the seaborne crude cap at $44.10** via the **21st Sanctions Package**. The design: cap Russia's revenue while keeping the barrels flowing.

## The shadow fleet (fact)
Russia's workaround is a **"shadow fleet" of ~600-700 tankers** (up from ~200-300) operating outside Western insurance/services, using **ship-to-ship transfers**, **re-flagging** in low-oversight jurisdictions, **non-coalition intermediaries** to break the compliance chain, and **falsified documentation** - tactics **modeled on Iran**. The result has been a narrowing Brent-Urals discount and barrels redirected to India/China on a parallel shipping-and-insurance stack.

## Gunvor and Russia (contested - both positions given)
Gunvor publicly pledged to exit Russian oil. But a **"strictly confidential" report** acknowledged its subsidiaries transacted with **sanctioned Russian counterparties (Gazprom/Rosneft/Lukoil/Novatek)** at **~0.69% of Q1-2024 group revenue (~$234M**, per a Goteborgs-Posten estimate). **Gunvor's official position:** it **stopped trading Russian crude** and handles only **"very small volumes"** of sanctions-compliant Russian oil **products**. Both the report figure and the company's denial are presented - **not resolved here.**

## The bribery track - a DIFFERENT matter (fact)
Kept explicitly separate because the sources do **not** link it to Russia-sanctions evasion:
- **Gunvor:** pleaded guilty, **>$661M** (US FCPA, **Ecuador/Petroecuador**, 2024) + ~$98M to Swiss authorities.
- **Vitol:** ~$90M (US) + ~$29M (CFTC) + ~$45M back to **Petrobras** (DPA, **Brazil**).
- **Trafigura:** guilty plea, **~$127M** fines + forfeiture (Latin-America bribery).

These are **Latin-America corruption** cases from before the shadow-fleet era. Conflating them with Russia-sanctions evasion would be a category error the block refuses.

## Honest limits
Cap mechanics, shadow-fleet scale, the FCPA settlements, and Gunvor's confidential-report figure **and** its official denial = **fact** (both Gunvor positions side by side). The corruption track is **distinct** from the Russia-sanctions track (the sources don't link them). **No claim** that any named house operates the shadow fleet - that architecture runs on opaque intermediaries and the dark fleet, not necessarily these firms.
