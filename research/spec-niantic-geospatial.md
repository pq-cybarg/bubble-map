# Niantic: from a CIA-seeded mapping startup to a crowdsourced 3D world - and who owns the location data now

John Hanke's career is the throughline of consumer geospatial surveillance.

## The lineage
- **Keyhole** (Hanke co-founded, 2001) - a satellite/aerial 3D-mapping startup **funded by In-Q-Tel (the CIA's venture arm) in 2003**, alongside NGA/NIMA (defense mapping). Used in early-2000s military ops.
- **Google acquired Keyhole (2004)** - its engine became **Google Earth** (2005) and fed Google Maps: the mass-consumer adoption of IC-seeded tech.
- **Niantic** - Hanke founded Niantic Labs inside Google (2010), spun out 2015. Shipped **Ingress** (2012) and **Pokemon GO** (2016).

> **Critical caveat (kept from the original block):** In-Q-Tel/NGA funded **Keyhole**, *not* Niantic. The intelligence tie is via Hanke's heritage only - this does **not** imply IQT funds Niantic or that any agency accesses Niantic data today.

## The games as a crowdsourcing engine
Ingress "portal" submissions and Pokemon GO gameplay drive **hundreds of millions of players** to physically visit, photograph, and 3D-scan real-world locations - continuous precise GPS + sensor + camera data. Aggregated, this builds Niantic's proprietary map + **Visual Positioning System**. Niantic acquired **Scaniverse** (3D-scanning app) to source high-fidelity scans directly.

## The real asset: spatial AI
**Nov 2024:** Niantic announced a **Large Geospatial Model** - AI trained on **~10M+ player-scanned locations** to reconstruct/understand the physical world for AR and robotics/navigation. The games were, functionally, a **data-collection front-end** for a spatial-AI foundation model.

## Who owns it now
- **Mar 2025:** Niantic **sold its games** (Pokemon GO, Ingress, Monster Hunter Now) to **Scopely for ~$3.5B**, keeping the geospatial AI as **"Niantic Spatial."**
- **Scopely** is owned by **Savvy Games Group**, a subsidiary of **Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund** (PIF, ~$4.9B, 2023) - so the world's largest real-world-location game now sits under a **foreign sovereign wealth fund**. That's a governance/access risk (interpretation grounded in the ownership fact), not an alleged breach.

## Honest limits
Player scanning is opt-in-ish (consent buried in gameplay); the concern is the **aggregate map + ownership**, not a specific leak. Location data broadly flows into the **data-broker** ecosystem (SDKs/ad exchanges) - a systemic risk around any large location dataset, wired here to the LexisNexis/auto-surveillance thread, not a claimed Niantic sale.
