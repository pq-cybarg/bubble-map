## 4. The broader claim (mixed)

Part I widens the aperture: **(a)** Chinese state media (CGTN/China Daily/Global Times) ran attributed anti-data-center items while China subsidizes its own AI-datacenter energy — the report reads the asymmetry as intent; **(b)** Russian **RT** promoted data-center bans in ME/NY/SC/OK; **(c)** foreign billionaires **Wyss** (Swiss, ~$700M) and **Parker** (British, Oak Foundation >$750M) fund US advocacy; **(d)** a **230+ org** coalition letter (Dec 8 2025) preceded a Sanders bill ~107 days later; **(e)** Singham-funded **Tricontinental** opposed US chip export controls echoing China's Foreign Ministry. *Grade: mixed — the state-media items and grants are documented, but Wyss/Parker are Swiss/British funders of broad progressive causes (folding them into "against American AI" is a stretch), and parallel messaging ≠ shown coordination.*

## 5. The $23.6B figure (weak)

Part III's headline number has **no stated methodology** and names only three dollar-figured projects (Prince George's MD **$5B**; DeForest WI **$12B**; Monterey Park CA ban, unspecified) — summing to ~**$17B**, with the remainder unexplained. *Grade: the specific blocked projects are real; the **$23.6B aggregate is unverified**.*

## 6. Publisher interest (read words-for-intent on the framer)

The **Bitcoin Policy Institute** is a pro-Bitcoin / pro-AI-infrastructure think tank, so it has a direct interest in recasting data-center opposition as foreign-influenced rather than legitimate local dissent. That doesn't falsify the documented facts (corroborated well beyond BPI), but the **selection and causal framing** — which opposition counts, how much agency to assign the network — should be read as advocacy and graded independently.

## 7. Composition & causation guard

The error to avoid: treating "documented Singham funding + CCP-aligned content + PSL participation in some campaigns" as "the American anti-data-center movement **is** a Chinese operation." The reports themselves disclaim it — Part II: *"This report does not assert that members of the PSL are Chinese operatives"*; Part III: *"Americans have genuine concerns... These concerns are authentic,"* with a foreign campaign running *"parallel to this domestic, democratic movement."* Genuine drivers (electricity prices, water, noise, land, tax abatements) are real and widespread. Warranted findings: **(i)** a real CCP-aligned Singham network exists; **(ii)** its activists genuinely joined specific data-center fights; **(iii)** whether that **directed** (vs joined/amplified) the movement is unproven, and the aggregate-impact figure is unsubstantiated.

## 8. The honest reading

There **is** a documented, CCP-aligned influence network around Singham (NYT 2023; IRS 990s ~$278M; Delhi "ultimate paymaster" 2024; Rubio FARA referral; reported DOJ grand-jury probe), and activists from that ecosystem **genuinely** took part in ~21 US data-center fights that produced moratoria, a ban, and withdrawals. That is fact. BPI's bigger claim — a coordinated **foreign influence campaign** that blocked **"$23.6B"** — is graded: the reports hedge causation, the dollar figure has no method, and the author is a pro-AI institute. By the **composition guard**, the finding is a real network + real participation + parallel messaging — **not** proof that foreign direction originated or controls the opposition. By **read-words-for-intent**, the network conspicuously does not disclose its financing (PSL is exempt from public reporting) and echoes CCP positions — which cuts toward alignment; but non-disclosure + alignment is **not** command. ## 9. The 2026 platform-level finding (OpenAI + X) — and why it doesn't erase the real backlash

The Singham thread above is a *claim about who funds activists*. Separately, in 2026 two platforms documented **coordinated inauthentic behavior (CIB)** directly:

Cluster 1, **"Data Center Bandwagon,"** produced English comments + comic strips + captioned electricity-marketing images claiming datacenter buildouts raise ordinary families' power bills. Cluster 2 attacked US tariffs (prompts explicitly **excluded Xi, featured Trump**). Operators prompted in **Simplified Chinese via VPN**; OpenAI assessed them as **likely a private Chinese tech company's social-media team working for provincial-government clients** — contractor-grade, *not* confirmed Beijing-directed — and that the campaign achieved **almost no authentic engagement.**
- **X, Aug 27 2026.** Its Safety team said it found **~200,000 China-linked inauthentic accounts**, ~200 posting datacenter comics/images "**in a manner that could manipulate a legitimate debate** about American AI and energy policy." Strongest corroboration: the **AI-generated artwork matches the illustrations in OpenAI's June report** (two independent investigations, same images). X **did not disclose reach.**
- **Context — Spamouflage/Dragonbridge** (separate, larger CCP op): Meta removed 7,704 FB accounts (2023); Google TAG disrupted 50k+/10k+ instances (2022/Q1-2024); Google TAG's verdict: "**practically no organic engagement from real viewers.**" Do not conflate it with the contractor-grade "Data Center Bandwagon."

**The authentic backlash is large and it is the signal the op rides:** ~$156B of projects delayed/cancelled in 2025; 75 projects worth >$130B delayed in Q1 2026; **61% of Americans** oppose datacenters in their area (UPenn, +12 pts since spring); 142 protests across 42 states (Jul 18 2026); **300+ localities** with bans/moratoria — driven by electricity-cost pass-through, water, noise, and land.

Per this project's composition/causation guard, **both moves are illegitimate:** a documented CIB existing does not make the movement foreign, and organized funding does not make a grievance fake.

**Honest reading (hold all three at once):** (1) a documented China-linked CIB targeting the US datacenter/energy debate **exists** (OpenAI + X, corroborated by matching artwork); (2) its measured impact is **tiny** (near-zero engagement, ~200 active accounts, undisclosed reach); (3) the authentic domestic backlash is **large, bipartisan, and grievance-driven.** The datacenter is the physical chokepoint where the AI-capital loop meets the grid and the public — so it is exactly where legitimacy (the map's center of gravity) gets contested.
